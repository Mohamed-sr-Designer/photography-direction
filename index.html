01 — Objective

Beyond Beautiful
Images.

Create a premium visual library that communicates the development's scale, location, architectural quality, investment value and lifestyle potential.

This document is a production brief, not a mood board. Every frame on it has a job to do in a real layout. Shoot the list, not the highlights.

The goal is not only to capture beautiful images. The goal is to create a complete visual asset library that gives the design team enough flexibility to build an entire digital ecosystem from the shoot.

02 — Volume Requirement

The Number That
Defines This Shoot.

36 images

36 images for every project, and 36 images for every type.

Each of the ten categories in this brief is a separate delivery of 36 final images. A category is not complete at 20 frames, and it is not complete when the same subject has been shot 36 times from one position. The 36 must spread across the composition matrix: wide, medium and close, landscape and portrait, with people and without, clean and environmental.

Ten categories at 36 images each is 360 final images per project. That is the delivery. Anything under it leaves the design team short of material and forces the same four images to carry the website, the campaign and the social calendar.

10 categories×36 images=360 images per project

04 — Photography Priorities

What Matters
Most.

If the shoot runs short on time or light, this is the order in which things get cut. Never cut from the top.

Altitude Ladder

Three heights, three jobs. Cover all three before moving to the next zone.

  1. High Establishing

    The whole development and everything around it. Answers “where is this”.

  2. Mid Planning

    Zones, roads, green areas. Answers “how is this organised”.

  3. Low Cinematic

    Close to the ground with a strong foreground. Answers “how does this feel”.

Composition Priority

Hero architectural images must provide enough negative space for headlines, statistics, call to action buttons and graphic overlays. A perfectly centred building with no empty area cannot be used as a hero image.

Land Is Photographed In Context

Empty ground photographed on its own reads as nothing. The same plot photographed with a road, a boundary marker, a service line or a finished building behind it reads as an opportunity. Every land frame needs at least one anchor.

Core Message

Show where the development is, how people reach it, and what surrounds it.

Shoot the arrival the way a buyer experiences it. Start on the main road, move to the turn off, then the entrance, then inside. That sequence becomes the location section of the website on its own.

  1. 01Main road
  2. 02Turn off / access point
  3. 03Approach
  4. 04Entrance
  5. 05Inside the development

The project is the hero. People provide scale, context and aspiration.

Shot Variety

Every outdoor space is captured at three distances from the same visit.

Visual Style

These frames are about credibility. Real plans on the table, real light in the room, real people looking at the work rather than at the lens.

These images create flexibility for website sections, social content, campaign layouts and supporting visual storytelling.

Overlap With Masterplan Is Intentional

Some subjects appear in both lists. The masterplan set is about the place. The drone set is about altitude, angle and variety. Both are counted and both are delivered. If two aerial frames look interchangeable, one of them is wasted.

Website · Social · Advertising

Where These
Images Go.

Every frame in this brief ends up in one of four places. The format requirements below are not preferences. A frame that cannot be cut to these ratios cannot be used.

Ratio Coverage Per Hero Subject

Every hero subject is captured in all four ratios on location. Cropping a 16:9 frame down to 9:16 loses the composition and the subject.

Composition Requirements

Variations,
Not Repeats.

For every important scene, capture variations across the four axes below. This is how one subject becomes six usable assets instead of one.

The single most important rule

Shoot wider than the final crop.

Images should allow the design team to crop and adapt them for multiple digital formats. Leave margin on all four sides of every frame. A tight composition that looks perfect in camera becomes unusable the moment it has to fit a different format.

Time Of Day

Light Plan
For The Day.

The must have list is shot in daylight. Golden hour and blue hour are short and cannot be recovered, so they are planned first and everything else is built around them.

Technical Requirements

How Files Are
Delivered.

Deliver clean, neutral, high resolution files. Grading, cropping and overlays are handled by the design team afterwards.
